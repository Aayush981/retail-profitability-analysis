\# E-Commerce Sales \& Profitability Analysis



\## Project Overview



High revenue does not always translate into strong profitability.



This project analyzes e-commerce sales data to identify the factors reducing overall profit despite generating approximately $2.3M in revenue. Using SQL and Power BI, the analysis focuses on discounting behavior, category-level profitability, product performance, and regional trends to uncover operational inefficiencies affecting business performance.



\---



\# Business Objective



The objective of this project was to:



\- Evaluate overall sales and profitability performance

\- Identify loss-making products and categories

\- Analyze the relationship between discounts and profit margins

\- Detect operational inefficiencies across regions and customer segments

\- Support profitability-focused business decision-making



\---



\# Dataset Information



\- \*\*Dataset:\*\* Sample Superstore Dataset (Kaggle)

\- \*\*Records:\*\* 10,000+ retail transactions

\- \*\*Domain:\*\* E-Commerce / Retail Analytics



The dataset includes sales, profit, discount, category, sub-category, region, customer segment, and order date information used to analyze business performance and profitability trends.

\# Key Business Insights



\### 1. High Revenue Did Not Translate into Strong Profitability



The business generated approximately \*\*$2.3M in revenue\*\* but only \*\*$286K in profit\*\*, resulting in a relatively low \*\*12.5% profit margin\*\*.



The findings indicate significant margin leakage driven by discount-heavy sales strategies and underperforming product categories.



\---



\### 2. Furniture Generated Revenue but Weak Profitability



Although Furniture contributed substantial sales volume, it delivered minimal profit compared to other categories.



This suggests that high sales performance alone is not a reliable indicator of business health when margins remain weak.



\---



\### 3. Tables and Bookcases Were Major Loss-Making Sub-Categories



Sub-categories such as \*\*Tables\*\* and \*\*Bookcases\*\* consistently generated negative profit despite continued sales activity.



These products represent concentrated sources of financial inefficiency and require pricing, discount, or cost structure review.



\---



\### 4. Aggressive Discounting Reduced Profit Margins



Profitability declined significantly when discounts exceeded the 20–30% range.



The analysis suggests that discount-driven sales growth increased revenue volume while simultaneously eroding margins.



\---



\### 5. Profit Growth Did Not Scale Proportionally with Sales Growth



Sales increased during peak periods, particularly toward year-end, but profit growth remained comparatively weak.



This indicates that higher sales volume alone was insufficient to improve overall business profitability.



\---



\# Business Recommendations



\### Optimize Discount Strategy



Implement discount thresholds and monitor profitability across discount tiers to prevent excessive margin erosion.



\### Reevaluate Loss-Making Products



Review pricing models, supplier costs, and promotional strategies for underperforming sub-categories such as Tables and Bookcases.



\### Prioritize High-Margin Products



Increase marketing and inventory focus on profitable sub-categories such as Copiers, Phones, and Accessories.



\### Improve Profitability Monitoring



Track both revenue and profit simultaneously to prevent sales growth from masking operational inefficiencies.
